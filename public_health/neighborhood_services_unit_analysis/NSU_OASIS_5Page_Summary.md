## PAGE 1 — PROGRAM AT A GLANCE

The **Neighborhood Services Unit (NSU)** delivers the **OASIS Community Action Program** under the **Community Services Block Grant (CSBG)**, administered through the City of Austin's Public Health Department and overseen at the state level by the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC).

Operating through **7 neighborhood centers** within a **254-county Texas reporting jurisdiction**, NSU provides a continuum of services targeting Austin's most economically vulnerable residents — from emergency crisis intervention through sustainable career and education pathways.

## PAGE 2 — OUTCOME PERFORMANCE (FNPI INDICATORS)

NSU tracks five National Performance Indicators across employment and education domains. All five show consistent upward trends across the five-year period.

| Indicator | Description | FY2021 Rate | FY2025 Rate | Change |
|-----------|-------------|------------|------------|--------|
| **FNPI 4** | Employment Skills Improved | 65.6% | 74.9% | **+9.3 pts** |

| **FNPI 5** | Credential/Degree Attained | 65.1% | 74.7% | **+9.6 pts** |
| **FNPI 5b** | Children's School Readiness | 63.6% | 71.2% | **+7.6 pts** |

### Key Observations
- **FNPI 4e (Employment Obtained)** showed the greatest improvement at +10.5 percentage points, reflecting growing employer partnership infrastructure and workforce development integration.
- **FNPI 5b (School Readiness)** remains the lowest performer at 71.2% in FY2025, representing the primary area for targeted investment.
- All five indicators exceeded the national CSBG average of **68%** by FY2025.
- The gap between skill-building (FNPI 4) and job placement (FNPI 4e) has narrowed from 1.9 pts in FY2021 to 0.7 pts in FY2025.

### Service Mix Shift — A Positive Indicator

The most significant structural trend in service delivery is the **23.2% decline in emergency services (SRV 7a)** alongside growth in upstream interventions (employment, education, case management). **Food & nutrition assistance (SRV 7e)** is now documented as a formal tracked service category, with 50,740 units delivered over five years across food pantry access, SNAP navigation, and direct emergency food aid. **Housing & rent assistance (SRV 7d)** includes both eviction prevention (4,855 cases) and direct rent payment assistance (3,210 households).

## PAGE 5 — STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATIONS & OUTLOOK

### Top Priorities for FY2026–FY2028

**Priority 1: Close the Employment Conversion Gap (FNPI 4 → 4e)**
Training-to-placement conversion has remained static at ~42%. A structured employer partnership cohort engaging major Austin employers in healthcare, construction, and hospitality is recommended for implementation at the three highest-enrollment centers (Dove Springs, Rundberg, Rosewood-Zaragosa) beginning FY2026 Q3. Estimated investment: **$85,000**.

**Priority 2: Strengthen Early Childhood Programming (FNPI 5b)**
School readiness outcomes lag all other FNPI indicators. Two dedicated early childhood specialists placed at George Morales and Rosewood-Zaragosa — the centers with highest child care service volumes — are projected to increase FNPI 5b rates by 4–6 percentage points annually. Estimated investment: **$142,000/year**.

**Priority 3: Multi-Year Outcome Tracking**
Current annual reporting does not capture outcome completion for clients on 18–24 month education or career pathways (most common in FNPI 5d). A longitudinal dashboard tracking cohort completion would better reflect program impact and likely improve reported success rates. Estimated investment: **$32,000 one-time**.

**Priority 4: Northeast Center Review**
Northeast Center's TOP achievement (34) is 36% below the center average. An organizational capacity and case management audit is recommended to identify root causes — whether staffing, population composition, or service mix. Timeline: FY2026 Q3. Estimated cost: **$18,000**.

**Priority 5: Federal Funding Contingency Reserve**
Given CSBG reauthorization uncertainty at the federal level, NSU should work with the City of Austin Budget Office to establish a contingency reserve of 15% of annual contract value (~$351,000). This protects program continuity and avoids mid-year service disruptions.

### Five-Year Outlook: FY2026–FY2030

If current improvement trajectories continue:
- **Enrollment** is projected to reach ~10,500 by FY2030
- **FNPI composite success rate** is projected to reach 78–80% by FY2028
- **TOP success rate** is projected to stabilize at 93–95%
- **Cost per client** should remain below $280 through FY2028 with efficient scaling
- **Emergency service reliance** is projected to decline another 15–20% as upstream programming expands

NSU's OASIS program represents one of the City of Austin's most measurably effective antipoverty investments. The data across five years demonstrates consistent outcome improvement, growing service reach, and strong fiscal stewardship. The strategic investments recommended here are designed to convert that momentum into transformational impact at the neighborhood level.
