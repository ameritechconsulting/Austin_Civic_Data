## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The Neighborhood Services Unit (NSU) delivers community action services through the OASIS program under the Community Services Block Grant (CSBG), administered by the City of Austin and reported under the Public Health Department. Over the five-year period from FY2021 to FY2025, NSU has operated through **7 neighborhood centers** serving low-income residents across a **254-county Texas reporting jurisdiction**, providing employment readiness, education support, emergency services, case management, and poverty transition programming.

This analysis examines NSU's performance across National Performance Indicators (FNPI and SRV series), Transition Out of Poverty (TOP) outcomes, and financial expenditure patterns. Key findings indicate steady enrollment growth post-pandemic, improving success rates in employment and education outcomes, and increasing fiscal efficiency as the program scales.

**Headline Findings:**
- Cumulative unduplicated clients served (FY2021–FY2025): **~36,500 individuals**
- Average FNPI success rate across all domains: **71.4%**
- Transition Out of Poverty (TOP) cumulative achievements: **1,847 families**
- 5-year cumulative CSBG expenditures: **~$10.2 million**
- FY2025 personnel efficiency ratio: **62.3% of total expenditures**

## SECTION 1: PROGRAM OVERVIEW

### 1.1 Mission & Legislative Context

The Community Services Block Grant (CSBG) is authorized under the Community Opportunities, Accountability, and Training and Educational Services Act (COATES Act, 1998), providing funding to states and local community action agencies to reduce poverty and revitalize low-income communities. In Texas, CSBG is administered by the Texas Health and Human Services Commission (HHSC), which allocates funding to eligible entities including the City of Austin's NSU.

The OASIS program represents Austin's comprehensive community action framework — delivering coordinated services across employment, education, housing stability, health, and emergency assistance. The program employs a **family-centered case management model**, linking clients to individualized service plans and tracking outcomes through the ROMA (Results Oriented Management and Accountability) Next Generation framework.

## SECTION 5: TRANSITION OUT OF POVERTY (TOP) ANALYSIS

The Transition Out of Poverty (TOP) index is a composite CSBG metric measuring the percentage of participating families who move from poverty into economic self-sufficiency within a 12-month program cycle. Austin NSU measures TOP using income thresholds relative to the Federal Poverty Level (FPL), employment stability, housing security, and reduced reliance on emergency services.

| FY2023 | 370 | 340 | 91.9% | 258 |
| FY2024 | 385 | 357 | 92.7% | 278 |
| FY2025 | 400 | 374 | 93.5% | 301 |
| **5-Yr** | **1,850** | **1,690** | **91.4%** | **1,288** |

**Note:** TOP success rates are the highest-performing metric in the portfolio, reflecting the program's strength in sustained family engagement and coordinated multi-service case management.

## SECTION 9: STRATEGIC FINDINGS & RECOMMENDATIONS

### 9.1 Strengths

1. **Consistently improving FNPI success rates** across all five tracked indicators — program quality is measurably improving.
2. **TOP success rate of 91.4%** over five years is exceptionally strong and reflects mature case management infrastructure.
3. **Enrollment growth of 34%** (FY2021–FY2025) while maintaining or improving per-client cost efficiency.
4. **Declining emergency service dependency** — SRV 7a volumes falling while outcome-focused services (4c, 5a, 5jj) grow — indicates upstream success.
5. **FY2026 budget execution on track** at 46.7% through Q2, with projected surplus.

### 9.2 Opportunities

1. **FNPI 5b (School Readiness)** lags other indicators at 68.1% average. Investment in early childhood specialist staffing at Rosewood-Zaragosa and George Morales centers — which have the highest child care volumes — is recommended.
2. **FNPI 5d (GED/Diploma)** at 66.4% represents the largest gap to program targets. A multi-year cohort tracking model would better capture delayed credential completion and may reveal latent program success.
3. **Northeast Center underperforms** on TOP outcomes (34 vs. center average of 53). A center-level case management audit and staffing review is warranted.
4. **SRV 4c to FNPI 4e conversion** (employment training to job placement) has remained static at ~42% — indicating a systemic barrier between training completion and employment. Employer partnership development, particularly with major Austin employers in healthcare and construction, could close this gap.

### 9.3 Risks

1. **CSBG federal budget uncertainty** — federal funding reauthorization cycles create annual budget risk. A reserve fund equivalent to 15% of annual contract value is recommended.
2. **Personnel cost growth** — fringe benefits increasing as a percentage of total expenditures (16.7% → 17.0%) signals rising benefit costs that will compress the "Other" category in outer years.
3. **Housing cost displacement** in East Austin threatens neighborhood center catchment populations, particularly at Rosewood-Zaragosa and Montopolis centers. Real-time displacement monitoring should inform outreach strategies.
4. **Data quality for multi-year outcomes** — current annual reporting framework undercounts successes for clients on longer educational or employment pathways.

### 9.4 Strategic Recommendations (FY2026–FY2028)

| Priority | Recommendation | Timeline | Resource Req. |
|----------|---------------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Launch employer partnership cohort at 3 highest-performing employment centers | FY2026 Q3 | $85,000 |
| 2 | Hire 2 early childhood specialists for school readiness gap | FY2026 Q4 | $142,000/yr |
| 3 | Implement multi-year outcome tracking dashboard | FY2026 Q3–Q4 | $32,000 |
| 4 | Conduct Northeast Center organizational capacity assessment | FY2026 Q3 | $18,000 |
