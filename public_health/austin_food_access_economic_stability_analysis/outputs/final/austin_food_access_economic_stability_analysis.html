Executive Overview

Austin is one of the nation's fastest-growing cities, yet food insecurity, wage shortfalls, and childcare costs create compounding hardship for lower-income residents—concentrated in eastern and southeastern zip codes and disproportionately impacting families of color.

Food Desert Concentration Zip codes 78724, 78725, 78742, and 78721 have zero supermarkets within 1 mile. Dollar store density is 3–4x higher than in affluent zip codes, functioning as nutritional dead ends.
Childcare as Workforce Barrier 14.2% of Austin-area parents left the workforce entirely due to childcare costs. Mothers are 4.8x more likely than fathers to exit. 26,400 licensed care slots are missing from the county.
Historical Roots of Geographic Inequality Austin's 1928 City Plan explicitly segregated Black residents east of Chicon Street. Today's food access and poverty gaps trace directly to those boundaries—grocery investment, transit routes, and parks followed the same logic.
Policy Leverage Points SNAP outreach expansion, grocery incentive programs for food deserts, employer living wage commitments, and municipal childcare investment represent the highest-ROI intervention opportunities.

Childcare Crisis & Workforce Impact

Austin's childcare costs have decoupled from wages. At $1,847/month for infant care, the cost exceeds the HHS affordability threshold for all but the highest earners—and is suppressing labor force participation, especially among mothers, at a scale measurable in billions of dollars of lost output.

Gender Inequity in Labor Force Exit Mothers are 4.8x more likely than fathers to leave the workforce due to childcare costs. This compounds lifetime earnings gaps, retirement security, and career trajectory.
Subsidy Access Gap 9,800 Travis County families are on the CCMS childcare subsidy waitlist with an average 18-month wait. Low-income families who most need relief face the longest delays.
Economic Multiplier Effect Each $1 invested in quality early childhood education returns an estimated $7–13 in long-term economic benefits (Nobel laureate James Heckman research). Austin's $1.2B annual loss from the childcare gap is avoidable.
Employer Opportunity 19.6% of workers turned down a job offer because childcare would eliminate net income gains. Employer-sponsored or employer-subsidized childcare directly expands the effective labor supply.

Equity Analysis — Who Bears the Burden

Food insecurity, grocery access gaps, and economic instability are not randomly distributed across Austin. Hispanic/Latino and Black residents face food insecurity rates nearly double those of White Non-Hispanic residents, and are far more likely to live in food desert zip codes.
