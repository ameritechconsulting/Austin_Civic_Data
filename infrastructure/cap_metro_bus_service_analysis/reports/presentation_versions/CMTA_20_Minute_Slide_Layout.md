### Layout

### Visual
Insert: output/figures/stop_tract_density_combined.png
Alt (scatter only): output/figures/stop_tract_density_scatter.png

### On-slide text
- 1,250,625 reports · Oct 2021–Apr 2026 · 290 Travis County tracts
- Every high-demand stop: ≥2,000 reports per 1,000 residents in its tract
- SEVERE GAP stops exist even at the city's highest-density locations
- Density alone does not explain the gap — the agencies' supply side is simply absent

KPI box:
Tracts 11.01–11.03 (Guadalupe/UT): 5,975–7,921 reports/1k residents

### Speaker note
Counter-argument pre-emption: if anyone claims the stops don't have enough documented need to justify investment, show this slide. The tracts surrounding every high-priority stop are Austin's highest-volume 311 zones. District 4 has the most severe gap AND the most phone-dependent population — meaning its true conditions are likely understated in the data.

### Slide title
Who Can Report and Who Cannot

### Layout
- Full-width stacked bar chart

### Visual
Insert: output/figures/311_digital_divide_by_district.png

### On-slide text
- Citywide: 62.9% phone · 35.8% digital
- District 4: 65% phone (highest dependency)
- District 9: 58% digital (most engaged)
- Phone-dominant districts produce fewer digital records — lower count ≠ fewer problems

### Speaker note
This is an evidence-quality argument, not a technology argument. When the primary accountability mechanism (311) requires digital access to produce a complete record, communities that rely on phone calls are systematically under-documented. District 4's high gap + high phone share means the real infrastructure deficit is likely worse than the data shows.

## Slide 11 - North Lamar Emergency Accessibility Action (1:30)
### Slide title
North Lamar (12300 block): Immediate Sidewalk Gap Escalation

### Layout
- Left: situation snapshot and urgency
- Right: action checklist with contacts

### On-slide text
Situation:
- Persistent sidewalk gaps continue to create disability-access and pedestrian life-safety risk.
- Feb 2026 pedestrian beacon installation improves crossing visibility but does not provide a continuous accessible path.

Action checklist:
1. File/confirm exact-location 3-1-1 request: "Sidewalk Repair/New Installation"
2. Mark as life safety and wheelchair-access failure
3. Escalate to North Lamar Boulevard Mobility Project: corridors@austintexas.gov
4. Request District 4 or District 7 emergency mobility intervention and interim accessible connection
5. File ADA complaints:
	- City Office of Civil Rights: 512-974-3451, officeofcivilrights@austintexas.gov
	- CapMetro ADA Coordinator: 512-389-7583, ccr-accessibility@capmetro.org

### Speaker note
If leadership asks what can be done immediately, this is the parallel pathway: 3-1-1 documentation, corridor-office escalation, council intervention, and formal ADA compliance channels.
