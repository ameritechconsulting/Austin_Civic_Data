# Transit Stop Baseline
## Public Comment Version (Plain Language)

Audience: Community meetings and public comment sessions  
Target length: 6-8 minutes

## Opening
Thank you for the opportunity to share this baseline. We reviewed public data to understand whether Austin bus stops are meeting basic rider needs.

## Disclaimer: How We Got Here

This analysis is a baseline built from public data, not a legal finding and not a full performance audit.

What we did:
1. Selected high-priority stops using service activity and amenity condition files.
2. Combined stop-level records with 311 complaint patterns and ADA-related records.
3. Built repeatable indicators for amenity gaps, complaint burden, safety share, and ticket-age persistence.
4. Assigned each analyzed stop to a council district and census tract using official boundary files.
5. Generated reproducible tables and charts from scripts so the process can be rerun and checked.

Important limits to state clearly:
- GTFS trip counts are a service proxy, not direct ridership.
- 311 reflects reported issues, not all conditions on the ground.
- Data timing and category definitions can differ across source systems.
- Spatial outputs depend on source boundary geometry and stop-coordinate quality.

Bottom line: this is a transparent, evidence-based starting point to guide action now.

## What We Found
### 1) Too many busy stops still lack basic protections
At the busiest stops:
- 80% are missing shelter
- 64% are missing benches

That means many riders are standing in heat, rain, and bad weather without enough protection.

### 2) Problems are concentrated in specific places
A smaller group of stops appears again and again in the data for complaints and unresolved issues.

This is important because it means targeted fixes can make a visible difference quickly.

### 3) Safety and accessibility concerns are ongoing
Safety concerns are a recurring part of complaints.
ADA-related records are also present in high numbers in the baseline period.

### 4) Complaint totals are still high across full years
The data shows large complaint volumes year after year.
We should not assume improvement from partial-year numbers alone.

### 5) Many complaints at top stops remain unresolved
At some of the highest-call stops, large shares of tickets remain open.

Examples from this run:
- 1609 Lavaca/17th (Midblock): 142 calls, 113 unresolved (79.6%)
- Guadalupe/16th Street: 138 calls, 116 unresolved (84.1%)
- Lavaca/4th: 135 calls, 106 unresolved (78.5%)

This is why closure-time tracking matters, not just complaint intake.

## Why This Matters for Riders
- Waiting conditions affect safety, dignity, and comfort.
- Missing benches and shelters hit seniors, disabled riders, and families hardest.
- Long-standing complaints reduce trust in public systems.

## What We Are Asking Decision-Makers to Do
1. Fix the top-risk stops first, starting with shelter and seating.
2. Use a joint CMTA-City closure tracker for unresolved high-call complaints.
3. Publish monthly progress updates so riders can see what changed.

## North Lamar Emergency Accessibility Action (12300 block)
This corridor remains a life-safety concern for pedestrians and riders with disabilities where sidewalk gaps persist.

Immediate steps:
1. File or confirm a 3-1-1 request for the exact segment.
	- Request type: Sidewalk Repair/New Installation.
	- Include: life safety risk, wheelchair-access failure, and reported crash/incident details.
2. Escalate to the North Lamar Boulevard Mobility Project at corridors@austintexas.gov.
3. Ask District 4 or District 7 offices to request an Emergency Mobility Improvement at the affected stop area.
4. Request interim access treatment while full construction is pending (temporary sidewalk connection or landing pad).
5. File formal ADA complaints:
	- City of Austin Office of Civil Rights: 512-974-3451 or officeofcivilrights@austintexas.gov
	- CapMetro ADA Coordinator: 512-389-7583 or ccr-accessibility@capmetro.org

## Close
This baseline is not the end of the work. It is a starting point for clear, measurable action.
