## Slide 5a
### Title
Three Stops Create Critical-Tier Legal Liability Right Now

### On-slide bullets
- 3 stops rated CRITICAL liability tier (composite score ≥ 80/100)
- 30.9 million cumulative rider-exposure-days during the disrepair period
- 282 open ADA-flagged tickets; 0 confirmed repairs from either agency
- Administrative closure pattern: 60 tickets closed with zero days of actual resolution

### Visual
- Insert: output/figures/public_safety_risk.png

### Speaker notes
- This is the liability exposure slide. Use it with legal counsel or policy staff in the room.
- The rider-exposure-days metric translates missing infrastructure into aggregate harm opportunity — each day a high-ridership stop lacks shelter or safe access, thousands of riders encounter that hazard.
- The zero confirmed repairs finding is key: both agencies have knowledge of these conditions (from 311 records) but no documented corrective action. This is the constructive notice threshold.
- Guadalupe/16th Street scores 99.4/100 — near maximum liability — because of 116 open ADA tickets and 22 administrative closures that show no repair occurred.

### Callout box
Constructive notice = knowledge + inaction.
All 15 stops have been in documented disrepair for 3.1–3.3 years with 0 confirmed repairs.

## Slide 9b
### Title
311 Report Density by Census Tract: The Demand Is Documented

### On-slide bullets
- 1,250,625 311 records analyzed · Oct 2021–Apr 2026 · 290 Travis County tracts
- Every high-demand stop sits in a tract with ≥2,000 reports per 1,000 residents
- Tracts 11.01–11.03 and 337 (Guadalupe/UT corridor): 5,975–7,921 reports per 1k
- District 4 = highest gap index (54.1) + highest phone dependency (65%) — least digitally able to report

### Visual
- Insert: output/figures/stop_tract_density_combined.png
- Alt (scatter only): output/figures/stop_tract_density_scatter.png

### Speaker notes
- This slide answers the counter-argument that low 311 volume means low demand.
- The tracts surrounding every high-demand stop are Austin's highest 311 density zones.
- The scatter plot makes the key point visually: SEVERE GAP stops exist even where density is extreme — the gap is not a reporting artifact.
- District 4's combination of high gap and high phone dependency means residents most harmed are least able to document it digitally.

## Slide 9c
### Title
Digital Divide: Who Can Report and Who Cannot

### On-slide bullets
- Citywide: 62.9% of 311 reports filed by phone · 35.8% digital (app/web)
- District 4: 65% phone — highest phone dependency of any district
- District 9: 58% digital — most digitally engaged (UT/young professional population)
- Phone-dominant districts have fewer data points in the record — lower counts ≠ fewer problems

### Visual
- Insert: output/figures/311_digital_divide_by_district.png

### Speaker notes
- This slide pairs with the equity report card: districts where residents rely on phone-only access produce systematically lower digital complaint records.
- If 311 data is the primary evidence base, phone-dominant districts are structurally under-documented.
- This is an evidence-quality argument: District 4's high gap index may understate true conditions.
- The F-across-the-board finding is not a reflection of one bad district — it reflects a system-wide failure to prioritize maintenance and safety at high-demand stops.
- Districts 3 and 4 carry comparable or worse outcomes to District 9 despite District 9 having the highest raw ticket volume — that speaks to under-reporting in lower-income areas.
- Use this slide to challenge any claim that equity is addressed through current operations.

## Design Notes
- Keep slides visually spare and legible.
- Use one main chart or one table per slide, not both, unless the slide is mostly verbal.
- Prefer large KPI numerals over dense paragraphs.
- For charts, crop or resize so labels are readable from the back of a room.
- If time is tight, present Slides 1, 3, 4, 5a, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9a, and 10.
