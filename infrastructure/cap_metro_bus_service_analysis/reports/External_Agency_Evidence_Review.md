## Purpose

This review examines agency-published documents for evidence of any capital investment, improvement plan, ADA compliance commitment, or repair action directed at the 15 high-demand bus stops identified in the gap analysis. The question driving this review is direct: **do agency documents confirm, commit to, or even acknowledge the infrastructure failures documented in the 311 and ADA datasets?**

## Findings by Document

### 1. City of Austin — FY2025–2026 Capital Improvement Recommendations (Planning Commission, May 13, 2025)

**18 of 39 matched snippets. Highest-yield document. Zero relevance to bus stop infrastructure.**

This memo is the City's formal annual capital improvement submission, required by City Charter Article X. The Planning Commission is mandated to submit a five-year capital improvement list to the City Manager by May 31 each year.

**What the document says about equity and safety:**

The memo invokes the *Imagine Austin* vision statement as its equity foundation:

> *"These capital improvements are intended to further the City's long-term vision and aspiration of being a 'beacon of sustainability, social equity and economic opportunity; where diversity and creativity are celebrated; where community needs and values are recognized; where leadership comes from its citizens and where the necessities of life are affordable and accessible to all.'"*
> — FY2025–2026 CIP Memo, p. 1

**What the document commits to:**

Capital improvement priorities cited in this document include:
- Continuation of Mobility Bond projects funded in 2016, 2018, and 2020
- Vision Zero safety improvements to address "disproportionate share of people of color among severe crash victims"
- Equitable Transit-Oriented Development (eTOD) station area improvements
- Commercial Stabilization Program enhancements (street banners, bike racks, landscape improvements in Cultural Districts)
- Parks and trails, stormwater retrofits, library facilities
- Support for Economic and Cultural District plans (East 12th Street, North Lamar International District, East Cesar Chavez, South Congress, African American Cultural Heritage District, 5th Street Mexican American Heritage Corridor)

**What the document does not mention:**
- Bus stop shelter installation or replacement
- Bus stop bench or accessibility upgrades
- ADA-compliant landing pad improvements
- Any of the 15 high-demand stops identified in this analysis
- CapMetro stop amenity standards or the Guadalupe corridor

**Significance:** The City's own five-year capital improvement recommendation — the legal mechanism for directing infrastructure spending — makes no mention of bus stop conditions despite 15 stops having accumulated a combined 17,745+ disrepair-days of unresolved 311 complaints. The equity commitment is aspirational. The capital deployment does not reach transit stop infrastructure.

## The Aspiration–Action Gap

The most analytically significant finding is the distance between stated commitments and observed capital deployment.

**Agency commitments, as stated in reviewed documents:**
- City of Austin: "social equity and economic opportunity…necessities of life affordable and accessible to all" *(CIP Memo, p. 1)*
- City of Austin: "Prioritize investments related to public health and safety" *(CIP Memo, p. 5)*
- City of Austin: "Expand Vision Zero safety improvements to address the disproportionate share of people of color among severe crash victims" *(CIP Memo, p. 2)*
- City of Austin: "Focus capital improvements…within areas intended to be compact and connected, such as Equitable Transit-Oriented Development station areas" *(CIP Memo, p. 2)*

**What the 311 and ADA data show at the same stops:**
- Mean disrepair duration across 15 stops: **1,183 days** (3.2 years per stop, on average)
- Stops with 1,000+ disrepair-days: **15 of 15** (100%)
- ADA complaints closed same-day without resolution: **100% every year, 2023–2026**
- Confirmed capital improvements or repairs documented in any agency source: **0**

The City's CIP process produces equity language annually. The stops most heavily used by transit-dependent, lower-income, and majority-minority riders in Districts 3 and 9 have not appeared in that process in any year reviewed.

## Implications for the Gap Analysis

The gap analysis (see `output/tables/gap_analysis_by_stop.csv`) assigned `planned_improvement_points = 0` to all 15 stops because no capital plan file was present. This evidence review confirms that zero is not a data gap — it is the correct value. No reviewed agency document allocates capital to these stops.

The `capital_source` field in the gap analysis output reads `no_capital_plan_file_found`. Based on this review, it could accurately be updated to `confirmed_zero_by_document_review`.

**Gap analysis results stand as published:**
- 15 of 25 stops: SEVERE GAP tier (gap index ≥ 25)
- Top gap stop: Guadalupe/16th Street (gap index 64.1)
- District with highest average gap: District 3

## Recommendations for Strengthening This Evidence Record

The following documents, if obtained, would directly address the remaining evidence gaps:

1. **CapMetro ADA Transition Plan or Self-Evaluation** — Required under 28 CFR §35.105 (Title II). Would confirm whether CapMetro has identified these stops as barriers and, if so, what remediation timeline is committed.

2. **City of Austin ADA Transition Plan (current version)** — Required under 49 CFR §27.11 (transit recipients). Would confirm the City's awareness of and commitment to accessible bus stop landings.

3. **CapMetro Bus Stop Standards and Amenity Policy** — Would establish the agency's own benchmark for what each stop should have, enabling a compliance gap against observed conditions.

4. **CapMetro Capital Improvement Program (CIP) or FY2026 Annual Budget** — Would provide the authoritative supply-side figure for the gap analysis's `planned_improvement_points` field.

5. **CapMetro Board Meeting Minutes referencing Guadalupe Corridor or high-demand stop improvements** — Would establish whether board-level awareness of these conditions exists.
