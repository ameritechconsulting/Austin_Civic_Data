Policy Recommendations

Prepared By: Ameritech Consulting Group

This document presents the full set of policy recommendations developed from Ameritech Consulting Group's analysis of Austin's Transportation & Public Works Department. Recommendations are organized by priority tier with lead assignments and timelines. They address the five key findings in the companion Findings Summary: growing capital backlog, unmet Vision Zero targets, racialized flood risk, persistent service inequities, and the absence of an anti-displacement framework paired with infrastructure investment.

Organizing principle: None of these recommendations require new budget authority in isolation — they require new policy direction. The tools, resources, and institutional capacity are largely in place. What is missing is a Council directive to apply them systematically.

High-Priority Recommendations — Immediate Action (0–18 Months)

High Priority Recommendation 1
Adopt an Equity-in-Infrastructure Framework
Lead: TPW + Equity Office
Timeline: 0–12 months

Establish disaggregated service standards for pavement condition (PCI), sidewalk coverage, drainage infrastructure age, and maintenance response time — broken out by neighborhood income quartile and race/ethnicity demographics. Require annual equity progress reporting to City Council. This creates the accountability baseline against which all future TPW performance is measured and makes service inequities visible and addressable as a policy matter rather than an operational byproduct.

High Priority Recommendation 2
Require Displacement Impact Review for Capital Projects >$5M
Lead: TPW + Planning Dept.
Timeline: 0–18 months

Mandate a Displacement Impact Review (DIR) — analogous to environmental review — before approval of any capital corridor project exceeding $5M in equity-priority geographies. Direct TPW and the Housing Department to jointly develop an infrastructure-affordability framework specifying mitigation options for projects triggering displacement risk thresholds. High-risk corridors include North Lamar, Airport Blvd, E. MLK Jr. Blvd, Rundberg Lane, E. William Cannon, and the Onion Creek watershed.

High Priority Recommendation 3
Accelerate Sidewalk Gap Closure in Equity Priority Zones
Lead: TPW Active Transportation
Timeline: Ongoing

Prioritize sidewalk buildout in census tracts overlapping the High Injury Network, transit corridors, schools, and lower-income geographies. Adopt an explicit annual target for linear feet of sidewalk gap closure in equity-priority zones. Report progress disaggregated by neighborhood demographics in each TPW annual report. The current inventory of 1,700–2,000 miles of sidewalk gaps represents a compounding public safety and equity failure — particularly for transit-dependent residents walking to stops on HIN corridors.

Medium & Long-Term Recommendations

Medium-Priority Recommendations (12–36 Months)

Medium Priority Recommendation 4
HIN Corridor Structural Redesign Commitment
Lead: TPW Vision Zero
Timeline: 12–36 months

Fund at least two full High Injury Network structural corridor redesigns per budget cycle — lane narrowing, raised pedestrian crossings, pedestrian refuge islands, protected bike facilities, and lighting upgrades. Require TPW to report annual fatality trends disaggregated by neighborhood income quartile and race/ethnicity to enable transparent accountability tracking against Vision Zero commitments. Incremental interventions (signage, paint, cameras) are necessary but are not substitutes for structural redesign.

Medium Priority Recommendation 5
Pair Flood Buyout Programs with Replacement Housing Plans
Lead: TPW + Housing Dept.
Timeline: 12–24 months

Require that all watershed buyout programs — beginning with Onion Creek — include a systematic replacement housing plan approved prior to the first property acquisition in each new buyout phase. Commission a Drainage Utility equity audit examining both fee structure and capital allocation patterns by income and race demographics. Link future buyout investments with ADCAP-style affordability preservation tools so that flood mitigation success is measured not only by properties removed from floodplains, but by residents able to remain in Austin.

Medium Priority Recommendation 6
Establish Community Benefits Agreement Template for Major Contracts
Lead: TPW + OPO
Timeline: 12–24 months

Develop a standard Community Benefits Agreement (CBA) template for all TPW capital contracts exceeding $5M, requiring local workforce hiring targets, small and minority business participation goals, and community liaison commitments. This converts major infrastructure contracts into local economic development tools — particularly important in HIN corridors and equity-priority neighborhoods where contract work is most concentrated.

Long-Term Recommendation (24–48 Months)

Long-Term Recommendation 7
Next Bond Cycle Equity Targeting Framework
Lead: TPW + City Council
Timeline: 24–48 months

Require that any future mobility bond program include an explicit equity allocation framework: a defined percentage or dollar floor for projects in equity-priority geographies, disaggregated performance targets as bond conditions, and an equity oversight committee with community representation. This ensures the structural lessons from the 2016 and 2020 bond programs — which created capital investment without displacement safeguards — are not repeated in the next cycle.
