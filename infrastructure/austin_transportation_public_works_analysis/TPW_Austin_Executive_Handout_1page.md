## WHO THEY ARE
Austin's **Transportation & Public Works Department (TPW)** — formed 2020 — is the city's largest operational department. It manages roads, bridges, drainage, sidewalks, traffic signals, and a $460M+ active bond program.

## 5 THINGS TO KNOW

**1. The maintenance gap is growing.**
Austin funds capital construction through bonds but chronically underfunds routine maintenance. The backlog grows ~$100–200M/year.

**2. Vision Zero targets were missed.**
Austin committed to zero traffic deaths by 2025. That goal was not met. The High Injury Network — 8% of streets, most crashes — runs through East and North Austin's lowest-income corridors.

**3. Flood risk follows poverty.**
Austin sits in Flash Flood Alley. Flood-prone areas are disproportionately lower-income and minority — a legacy of exclusionary zoning that pushed affordable housing into floodplains.

**4. Infrastructure investment can displace.**
Corridor improvements raise property values. Without affordability protections, bond-funded street and safety projects accelerate gentrification and displacement in East and North Austin.

**5. Federal dollars are flowing — but window is closing.**
The Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act (IIJA) unlocked major federal grants for Austin. Competitive grant cycles are time-sensitive; capture depends on project readiness and equity documentation.

---

## THE EQUITY GAP

> Austin has no systematic policy to pair major public works investment with affordability preservation. Every corridor project, flood buyout, and safety retrofit is a potential displacement trigger without a complementary anti-displacement framework.

**High-risk corridors**: North Lamar, Airport Blvd, E. MLK Jr. Blvd, Rundberg Ln, E. William Cannon, Onion Creek watershed (SE Austin)

---

## WHAT COMES NEXT

- Displacement Impact Review requirement for capital projects
- Equity-disaggregated service standards (PCI, sidewalk, drainage by neighborhood income/race)
- ADCAP-linked affordability preservation on infrastructure corridors
- Next bond program (anticipated): equity targeting framework needed
