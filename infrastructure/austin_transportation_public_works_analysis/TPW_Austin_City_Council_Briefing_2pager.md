# Austin Transportation & Public Works
## City Council Briefing
### Ameritech Consulting Group | May 2026

---

## SITUATION

Austin's Transportation & Public Works Department (TPW) manages the city's most critical physical infrastructure — roads, bridges, drainage, sidewalks, and traffic systems — against a backdrop of rapid population growth, a multi-billion-dollar capital backlog, and persistent service inequities between wealthier and lower-income neighborhoods.

The 2020 merger of Austin Transportation and Austin Public Works created a unified department with significant authority and resources. The 2020 Mobility Bond ($460M) and federal IIJA funding have injected capital into the system. But structural challenges remain: deferred maintenance is accumulating, Vision Zero fatality goals were not met, flood risk is racialized and growing, and infrastructure investment corridors are accelerating displacement without policy safeguards.

## THE FOUR CHALLENGES

### 1. CAPITAL BACKLOG ($3–5B+)
Austin's infrastructure is aging faster than the maintenance budget can address. The gap compounds annually. Bond programs fund new construction but do not adequately address maintenance of existing assets. Without a dedicated maintenance funding mechanism, each new bond cycle inherits a larger deferred maintenance burden.

**Council action needed**: Direct TPW to develop a lifecycle asset management plan with explicit maintenance funding targets and report annually to Council.

---

### 2. VISION ZERO — MISSED TARGETS, HIGH-RISK CORRIDORS
Austin adopted Vision Zero in 2016. ~100–120 residents die in traffic each year. The High Injury Network — concentrated on East and North Austin corridors serving low-income, transit-dependent residents — has not been fundamentally redesigned.

Speed, road geometry, and pedestrian infrastructure gaps are the primary structural drivers. Incremental interventions (signage, paint) have not moved the needle. Structural corridor redesign — narrowing travel lanes, building pedestrian refuge islands, installing protected bike facilities — is necessary but costly and politically contested.

**Council action needed**: Prioritize at least two full HIN corridor redesigns per budget cycle; require TPW to report annual fatality trends disaggregated by neighborhood income quartile.

---

### 3. FLOOD INFRASTRUCTURE — EQUITY AND DISPLACEMENT
Austin's flood-prone areas are disproportionately lower-income and minority. The Onion Creek and Williamson Creek corridors are flash-flood hazards where affordable housing stock is concentrated. The Onion Creek voluntary buyout program has relocated hundreds of households — but without systematic replacement housing, displaced residents often leave Austin entirely.

New development in upper watersheds is increasing impervious cover and worsening downstream flood peaks. The Drainage Utility Fund (~$80–100M/year) is the primary financing mechanism, but fee structures and capital priorities do not fully account for cumulative equity impacts.

**Council action needed**: Require that all watershed buyout programs include a replacement housing plan; examine Drainage Utility fee equity; prioritize drainage capital in historically underserved watersheds.

---

### 4. INFRASTRUCTURE AS A DISPLACEMENT VECTOR
Every major TPW capital project — corridor improvements, sidewalk buildout, drainage upgrades, trail extensions — increases adjacent property values. In a city without adequate affordability protections, public investment accelerates gentrification.

Austin currently has no systematic mechanism to evaluate displacement risk before approving capital projects, nor a policy requiring that infrastructure investment corridors be paired with affordability preservation tools.

**Council action needed**: Adopt a Displacement Impact Review (DIR) requirement for all capital projects exceeding $5M in displacement-sensitive areas; direct TPW and Housing Department to develop a joint infrastructure-affordability framework.

## RECOMMENDED COUNCIL DIRECTIVES

1. **Adopt Equity-in-Infrastructure Framework** — disaggregated service standards by income/race; annual equity progress report to Council
2. **Require Displacement Impact Review** — for all capital projects >$5M in equity-priority geographies
3. **HIN Corridor Redesign Commitment** — fund at least 2 full structural redesigns/year; report fatality trends by neighborhood income
4. **Drainage Equity Audit** — assess Drainage Utility capital distribution and fee structure for equity impacts
5. **Infrastructure-Affordability Linkage** — joint TPW + Housing directive to pair corridor investment with ADCAP-style affordability preservation tools
6. **Next Bond Cycle Equity Targeting** — require that any future mobility bond include an explicit equity allocation framework
