Here’s the main data you can analyze:

1. IRS Nonprofit Financial Data (Most Important)
Because they are a nonprofit, they file IRS Form 990s.
This gives:
* Revenue
* Salaries
* Grants
* Contractors
* Executive pay
* Major program expenses
* Board members
* Assets and liabilities
Main source:ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer
You can analyze:
* Revenue growth over time
* Funding sources
* Compensation trends
* Consulting/legal expenses
* Real estate/assets
* Government grant dependency

2. City of Austin Contract Data
You can check whether they:
* Received city contracts
* Participated in city-funded projects
* Were subcontractors
* Received grant funding
Main datasets:
* Austin Finance Online
* City procurement databases
* City council agenda backups
* Contract approval records
Official City portal:City of Austin Open Data Portal
Things to analyze:
* Total dollars awarded
* Which departments used them
* Repeat contract patterns
* Vendor relationships
* Equity/community engagement spending

3. Grants and Foundation Funding
Nonprofits often receive:
* Philanthropic grants
* Federal grants
* Community development funding
Sources:
* Candid/GuideStar
* Foundation annual reports
* HUD funding records
* EPA/community grants
* National Endowment grants
Official nonprofit research source:Candid / GuideStar
Analysis ideas:
* Which foundations fund them
* Network mapping
* Equity initiative funding
* Environmental justice funding
* Housing/displacement funding

4. Board Members and Leadership Networks
You can map:
* Board members
* Prior employers
* Political appointments
* Other nonprofits
* City commissions
* Consulting relationships
Useful for:
* Network analysis
* Influence mapping
* Public-private overlap
Sources:
* LinkedIn
* 990 filings
* Annual reports
* City commission records

5. Geographic Project Analysis
You can analyze:
* Which neighborhoods they work in
* Demographic overlap
* Gentrification/displacement areas
* Infrastructure investment overlap
Possible tools:
* GIS
* Census data
* Austin growth maps
* Project overlays
This becomes very powerful when combined with:
* Tax increment reinvestment zones
* Housing affordability data
* Transit investments
* Bond projects

6. Public Engagement / Equity Analysis
A lot of organizations like this operate in:
* Community engagement
* Equity planning
* Public workshops
* Participatory planning
You can analyze:
* Which communities are repeatedly targeted
* Funding tied to “engagement”
* Consultant ecosystems
* Equity initiative spending

7. Political and Policy Influence
You can examine:
* Council presentations
* Public testimony
* Bond campaign involvement
* Coalition memberships
* Advocacy participation
Sources:
* Austin City Council archives
* Meeting videos
* Agenda backups
* Public comments
Official site:Austin City Council Meeting Archive

8. Vendor Ecosystem Analysis
You can map relationships between:
* Nonprofits
* Consultants
* Engineering firms
* Equity consultants
* Community engagement vendors
* Universities
* Public agencies
This can reveal:
* Repeated collaborations
* Procurement concentration
* Shared grant ecosystems
* Public-private networks

9. Employment / Staffing Analysis
You can analyze:
* Job postings
* Required credentials
* DEI/equity staffing
* GIS/planning roles
* Community engagement staffing
Sources:
* LinkedIn
* Indeed
* Their website
* Annual reports

10. Public Perception / Media Analysis
You can review:
* News coverage
* Community criticism/support
* Neighborhood reactions
* Public comments
* Social media positioning
Sources:
* Austin Monitor
* Community Impact
* KUT
* Reddit
* Twitter/X

A strong analysis project could combine:
* Financial data
* Contract data
* GIS maps
* Demographic overlays
* Council records
* Vendor relationships
That creates a much more complete picture than looking at one dataset alone.
